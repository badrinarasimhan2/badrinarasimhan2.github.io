RESEARCH VISION

From matrix dynamics to biological organization.

Biological systems emerge from interactions across scales. My research seeks to define how dynamic material and extracellular cues are sensed, integrated, and translated into changes in migration, cell state, multicellular organization, and tissue behavior.

These principles are then reconstructed in engineered microenvironments to create predictive and controllable models of complex biological systems.

RESEARCH THEMES

Understanding interactions to build better biological systems.

Biological behavior emerges from interactions between cells, materials, molecules, and their environment. My research investigates how these interactions are coupled across scales, and how understanding them can help us build more predictive materials, organoids, and models of disease.

01

ECM–Immune Interfaces

How does the environment around a cell change the way the immune system sees it?

Cells communicate with the immune system while continuously sensing and remodeling their surrounding matrix. I study how these processes work together to shape immune interactions.

Why it mattersBetter models for studying cancer–immune interactions and therapeutic response.
02

Multicellular Organization

What tells cells where to go and how to organize?

Cells respond simultaneously to adhesion, mechanics, degradation, and neighboring cells. I study how these interacting cues generate migration patterns and multicellular architectures.

Why it mattersBuilding organoids with more predictable structures and behaviors.
03

Enzyme-Programmed Materials

Can cells reshape a material while the material reshapes the cells?

Enzymatic degradation does more than remove matrix. It can change properties such as stress relaxation, architecture, and confinement. I study these coupled changes to engineer materials that evolve with cells.

Why it mattersBiomaterials that dynamically adapt rather than remain passive.
04

ECM & Disease Progression

How does a changing tissue environment accompany disease progression?

I connect engineered models with patient tissues to understand how matrix remodeling and cellular states change together during disease.

Why it mattersMore physiologically relevant models for studying disease transitions.

EMERGING RESEARCH DIRECTIONS

Measure, map, predict, reconstruct.

Building from dynamic biomaterials and mechanobiology toward multimodal approaches that resolve how matrix and biological states evolve across space and time.

01

Mapping biological state spaces

Integrating spatial transcriptomics, spatial proteomics, tissue architecture, and extracellular matrix features to define how molecular and structural states are organized across tissues.

02

AI-guided state-space discovery

Developing data-driven and AI-enabled approaches to identify relationships among matrix properties, molecular states, cellular phenotypes, and tissue organization.

03

Visualizing dynamic matrix remodeling

Developing imaging-based approaches to track matrix displacements over time and quantify how local degradation produces time-dependent mechanical relaxation.

04

Reconstructing biological states

Using engineered extracellular matrices to reconstruct predicted microenvironmental states and test how they regulate cellular and multicellular behavior.

ABOUT

Badri Narayanan Narasimhan, Ph.D.

I am a Postdoctoral Scholar in Bioengineering at the University of California, San Diego.

My research investigates how extracellular matrix remodeling regulates cellular organization, mechanosensation, and disease progression. I integrate engineered biomaterials, mechanobiology, quantitative modeling, and spatial profiling to understand how matrix remodeling, mechanics, and adhesion shape cellular states.

My research trajectory spans dissipative hydrogel design, collagen microarchitecture and degradation, degradation-induced stress relaxation, and the reconstruction of disease-relevant microenvironments.
